System overview

SmartPark is a plate-first parking system. It receives camera events and video, recognizes vehicles, validates entry and exit against parking state and policy, records receipts/payments, and may command physical lane hardware. Recognition is evidence—not authority to open a barrier.

At a glance. QY/HVX cameras use a 32-bit Windows vendor-SDK host. Generic RTSP/ONVIF cameras use MediaMTX or an eligible direct fallback. A 64-bit Site Service coordinates recognition with parking policy and persistent state. SQLite supports one-computer sites; multiple processing nodes require shared PostgreSQL. There is no offline synchronization of authoritative parking sessions between edge nodes.

Entry flow

  1. Read a plate from a native camera event or a fresh sampled frame.
  2. Normalize and validate the plate; fuse readings if configured; persist vehicle snapshot and plate crop.
  3. Resolve lane and entitlement, then claim the site session idempotently.
  4. Apply receipt policy and print a receipt/QR for casual parking when needed.
  5. Only after policy and hardware checks, issue a recorded barrier command; passage updates session state.

Exit flow

  1. Read the plate or scan the receipt QR and locate the matching active session.
  2. Calculate the fee from the session tariff and policy; check entitlements and verified payments.
  3. If unpaid, keep the barrier closed and return payment instructions when available.
  4. After authorization, atomically claim one gate command and record the result.
  5. Confirm vehicle passage and close the session; replayed events cannot claim a second exit.

Strengths

  • Clear boundary between vendor camera SDKs, recognition, parking policy, and actuators.
  • Bounded latest-frame buffers and generation checks limit stale-frame and backlog issues.
  • Event, session, payment, and gate-command idempotency protect against duplicate actions.
  • Node/camera scope, audit trails, explicit simulation, and fail-closed checks support safer operation.
  • Evidence snapshots and health tools help diagnose freshness, drops, reconnects, and ownership.

Weaknesses and limits

  • Operationally complex: Windows x86/x64 runtimes, proprietary DLLs, MediaMTX, FFmpeg, ONNX models, and installers.
  • Large central modules make changes and review harder (app/api_main.py and app/web/index.html were noted as particularly large).
  • No supported offline synchronization of parking sessions between edge nodes.
  • Unit tests cannot establish real codec compatibility, OCR accuracy, night performance, gate reliability, or soak stability.
  • Some README links were found to target missing files in the reviewed checkout; confirm documentation against the current branch.
  • Shared database/site-service availability is important for cross-lane decisions and payments.

Camera video → display → recognition

Display and recognition are separate consumers. A monitor showing a camera does not trigger OCR; recognition consumes its own frame or native-event path. One source owner per camera avoids competing streams.

Important distinction. QY/HVX video decode and native plate callbacks are independent outputs. Generic cameras can use MediaMTX LIVE and DETECT roles. The Site Service coordinates accepted recognition evidence with parking state; OCR alone never authorizes gate operation.

Coordination rules

  • Worker ownership requires enabled features, camera-to-node assignment, registered DETECT path, and fresh per-process/per-camera heartbeat, frame, and inference evidence.
  • Bounded latest-frame mailboxes drop backlog. Results carry camera, sequence, generation, and vehicle-visit identity; reconnect invalidates old-generation results.
  • Worker loss returns eligible software recognition to the Site Service. QY/HVX stays on its SDK source owner.
  • Only after event delivery, capture persistence, entitlement/receipt/payment policy, session transition, and a unique command claim may an actuator be requested.

Policy values documented in the reviewed worktree: worker heartbeat health limited to 6 seconds; hybrid candidates pair within 3 seconds and wait up to 1.5 seconds. These are configuration/software policy values, not measured camera-to-screen or OCR latency. Field codec, accuracy, and recovery measurements are still required.

What to do next: recommended resilient architecture

Recommendation based on the current media, recognition, and lane-deployment documentation. Keep the existing per-gate ownership model and improve its recovery, observability, and persistence boundaries; avoid a wholesale rewrite or adding a second competing camera reader.

Recommended direction: a hybrid edge-and-central design. Each gate has a dedicated local processing node that owns its cameras, display streams, recognition, evidence journal, and lane devices. A central Site Service with PostgreSQL owns shared parking sessions, tariffs, payments, and cross-gate coordination. Video stays on the lane network; only recognition events and selected evidence cross to the central authority.
Durability is not the same as recording every video frame. Keep live queues bounded and latest-frame-oriented so latency cannot grow without limit. Persist recognition events and selected vehicle/plate evidence durably. If continuous video retention is required for operations or regulation, use a separately sized NVR/object-storage recording path with explicit retention and bandwidth planning; do not use the OCR queue as a recorder.

Build in this order

  1. Baseline and instrument: record installed versions and per-camera source/decode/display/recognition health; verify the current suite and current installer status.
  2. Prove single-gate recovery: real cameras, worker restart, stream reconnect, Site API/database loss, power/reboot, and a sustained soak.
  3. Harden event durability: if outage-safe event delivery is required, implement and test the local disk journal plus idempotent central acknowledgement before changing parking behavior.
  4. Prove multi-gate operation: PostgreSQL with unique node identity, assigned gate ownership, shared evidence policy, and concurrent entry/exit acceptance tests.
  5. Add high availability only to a measured need: database/API redundancy and automatic failover require fencing, recovery drills, and clear ownership—not just extra replicas.

Do not do these things

  • Do not let desktop/browser viewers open independent camera RTSP sessions when MediaMTX or the SDK is the assigned source owner.
  • Do not equate camera login, incoming bytes, process heartbeat, or a frozen last image with healthy decoded video.
  • Do not queue every video frame for OCR or let an unbounded queue increase recognition latency.
  • Do not authorize exit payment or barrier action from OCR alone.
  • Do not claim offline operation until local parking authority and conflict-resolution rules are implemented and acceptance-tested.

Reliability measures to define per site

Set numerical targets with the operator and hardware supplier before rollout; the source docs do not provide field-proven SLOs. Track:

  • Video: decoded frame age and FPS, display render age, reconnect count, time to recover, and camera-to-display latency per lane.
  • Recognition: arrival-to-first-candidate and arrival-to-consensus latency, labeled plate accuracy by conditions, false-read/review rate, and stale-generation rejection count.
  • Events: journal depth/age, retry count, central-commit delay, duplicate suppression, disk capacity remaining, and unreconciled evidence count.
  • Parking/control: API/database availability, payment reconciliation lag, gate command/result audit, and manual/degraded decisions.

Implementation boundary: current documentation already describes one source owner per camera, separate LIVE/DETECT paths, bounded frames, per-camera worker health/fallback, native/software fusion, generation/visit checks, and idempotent recognition delivery. The disk-journal outage design, central HA, and offline parking authority above are recommendations, not claims that those capabilities are already implemented.
